B8 · FOCUS STATE /the one permitted box-shadow Landed
box-shadow: 0 0 0 2px var(--void), 0 0 0 4px var(--text-primary);
A double ring: a 2px void gap that separates the control from the indicator, then a 2px bone ring that carries the contrast. It survives on any surface in the ramp because it does not depend on the background. It appears on :focus-visible only, so a pointer click stays quiet. Never remove it, never replace it with a colour, never trade it for a border change.

C1 · NEGATIVE SPACE IS SPECIFIED

Emptiness here is a declared value, not what was left over. The header clears clamp(64px, 11vw, 164px) before the statement begins, and every band after it is separated by clamp(56px, 9vw, 124px). Nothing spans the full twelve columns except the header and the one rule: the statement holds seven, the marginalia four, the exhibit plate nine. The display is additionally capped at 15ch, so it usually stops short of its own column and the rag stays deliberate.

If a future band feels cramped, take space from the content, not from the interval. The interval is the product's tone of voice.
C2 · THREE REGISTERS, NO FOURTH

One display voice (Geist Sans 600, clamp(38px, 6.2vw, 64px) at -0.06em, reprised quietly at 32px for the access heading). One lede (16 → 18px, muted, capped near 33 characters). One mono recording voice carrying every label, the marginalia, the exhibit caption, the specification strip and the honesty note. Marketing runs without an eyebrow class — the mono voice already does that work, and adding it would make a fourth register for no gain. Button labels are chrome, not a register.

The landed display ceiling of 64px is respected here. The 80px proposal from B3b is deliberately not used on this surface.
C3 · WHY MARKETING CARRIES NO HAIRLINES

The composition contains exactly one structural rule — above the specification strip, where the surface changes from persuasion to evidence and the reader should feel the register change. Only one other border exists, around the reserved plate, and it is functional rather than structural: remove it and an absent asset becomes an invisible one. Continuity is carried instead by a single left edge shared by the mark, the statement, the lede, the exhibit caption, the specification labels and the access heading.

Section D inverts this. A console is a table under scrutiny; it earns its rules, and hairlines there are load-bearing. Do not carry that density backwards into marketing.
C4 · THE CTA RULE

One action exists on this surface: Request Access (the only permitted variant is Apply for Access). It appears twice and is filled once — the header instance is borderless text, and the single bone block is held back until the access band, so the room brightens only at the door. There is no secondary action, no navigation list, no urgency device, no testimonial and no social proof, because an invite-gated beta has nothing to funnel.

Forbidden on this surface: a paired CTA cluster, a demo request, a pricing entry point, a sign-up or log-in pair, and any dropdown navigation.

D3 · RULES, NOT SPACE

Section C carries one structural rule in its whole height. This surface carries one every 45 pixels, and each of them is doing work: it separates two measurements that must not be read as one. Where marketing bought hierarchy with interval, the console buys it with alignment — a shared left edge for titles, a shared right edge for numerals, and a single header band at --surface-200. Nothing is centred, nothing floats, nothing is boxed for emphasis.

Density is not compression. Row padding stays at 12px vertical so ten rows can be scanned in one pass; what was removed is decoration, not air. If a future band feels crowded, delete an element — do not shrink the type.
D4 · NO CARDS, NO DASHBOARD

The obvious failure here is a row of KPI tiles above the table — four boxes, four big numbers, four coloured deltas. It is forbidden on this surface. A tile makes a number look like a headline; this product sells the opposite claim, that each number is a measurement with a trail. Also excluded: sparklines, progress arcs, trend arrows, coloured status pills, zebra striping, and any card that exists only to give a figure a frame.

The only two filled shapes in the whole console are the HOT chip and the Competition segments, and both pair with a literal word. Everything else is ink on the void behind a hairline.

D6 · ABSENCE, AND THE DIFFERENCE FROM WITHHOLDING /four marks, four meanings Proposed
— Not applicable The field does not apply to this row. HOT on a row scoring 89; an example link on a row with no eligible video. Quiet ink, because there is nothing to read.
n/a Not computable The field applies, but the data collected cannot produce it. A median needs at least one valid video; with none, velocity has no value to report and none is invented.
0 Measured zero A result, not an absence. Zero valid videos were found, and that is the finding. It carries full ink for exactly that reason — dimming it would file a measurement under “missing”.

The attached explanation, open
Score 83 or below is not published. A score was computed for this artist under the same rubric as every other row; it sits under the publication threshold and is withheld. It is not zero, and it is not missing data.
A blank cell is the most expensive thing a data product can print, because the reader cannot tell a rule from a bug. So no cell is ever blank here: each of the four cases prints a mark, and each mark is defined in the footnotes under the table it appears in. The distinction is Proposed — no landed authority defines these marks.
D7 · COMPETITION ENCODING /segments AND label, monochrome Proposed
Low ≤ 5 distinct channels published for the artist in the 30-day window.
Medium 6 to 15 distinct channels.
High More than 15 distinct channels, or publication in the last 7 days more than 50% above the previous 7 days.
Two encodings carrying one meaning: position (segments filled) and language (the literal word). Either one alone would fail — position alone is unreadable to anyone who has not learned the key, and a colour alone fails greyscale, low vision and print. A hue candidate for High exists and is quarantined in Section E as conflict C-2; it is not adopted here and this monochrome form is the default.

E6 · COMPONENTS THAT DO NOT EXIST /absence by decision Landed
  • No sidebar and no navigation taxonomy. The product is two reports and one door. A nav invents an information architecture that does not exist yet.
  • No modal, drawer or dialog system. The one disclosure in the product — the row trace — expands in place, which keeps the evidence beside the number it explains.
  • No pagination. Ten rows is the report. Paging a fixed ten-row record would imply there is more behind it.
  • No toasts or notification layer. Nothing in this product happens outside the reader’s view, so nothing needs to be announced from a corner.
  • No destructive-action grammar. The reader cannot delete, reset or regenerate anything. A confirm-to-destroy pattern with nothing to destroy is a liability, not a component.
  • No skeletons, spinners or empty-state illustrations. The report is sealed before it is read; there is no loading state to design, and the honest empty case is a row that prints 0.
Each of these would be easy to draw and impossible to justify. A component library is a claim about what the product does; inventing one ahead of the product is how a design system starts lying. When one of these is genuinely needed, it gets designed then — against a real requirement, not a template.

F6 · MOTION & REDUCED MOTION /one curve, short, one direction Landed
Easing
--ease-decided: cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1) and nothing else on this surface. It leaves fast and settles slowly, so a movement reads as decided rather than negotiated. --ease-overlay is landed as well, but there is no overlay in this product yet, so it is not used here.
Duration
150–250ms. Below 150ms the change is a flicker; above 250ms the reader waits for the interface. Everything in this file sits at 150ms for state changes, or 180ms for the lift on the primary action.
Distance
translateY 6–8px for an entrance, and 1px for the lift. Nothing scales, nothing rotates, nothing travels sideways, and nothing moves further than 8px — a document does not slide.
Forbidden

F8 · WHAT IS NOT VERIFIED /the honest list Open
  • No screen reader was run. The ARIA roles carried through the container-query reflow, the aria-expanded on report rows, the off-screen captions and the aria-describedby wiring are all reasoned from the specification, not tested with NVDA, JAWS or VoiceOver. Table semantics under display:block are the highest-risk item on this list.
  • No automated checker was run. No axe, no Lighthouse, no contrast tooling. Every ratio in F7 is hand arithmetic on the declared token values, and hand arithmetic is exactly as reliable as whoever did it.
  • Hairlines do not meet 3:1. --alpha-200 on the void measures 1.22:1, and the unfilled Competition segment at --alpha-300 measures 1.38:1. Section D calls its hairlines load-bearing, so this is a real finding rather than a technicality. Raising them would change the tone of the whole console, which makes it a Product-Lead decision and not one this artifact should take.
  • Forced colours will break the ring. Under @media (forced-colors: active) a box-shadow is discarded, and the focus indicator goes with it. There is no fallback in this file, and Windows High Contrast Mode was not tested.
  • Two targets are undersized. The tooltip trigger is 14×14 CSS px and the withheld mark is inline text, both below the 24×24 minimum of WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8. Neither was redesigned here, because both belong to a component set that is still Proposed.
  • Zoom and reflow were not checked. The layout was verified at 390, 834, 1280 and 1440 CSS px at 100%. SC 1.4.4 text resize to 200% and SC 1.4.10 reflow at 320px were not exercised.
  • Neither typeface is present. No Geist Sans or Space Mono binary exists in this repository and network fetch is forbidden, so every specimen renders in a local fallback. Any judgement that depends on letterforms — including SPIKE-F1, the tabular-figure question underneath conflict C-1 — cannot be made from this render, and the ratios above assume a stem weight the real faces may not have.
  • Nothing here is scripted. The opened row trace, the tooltip and the +/− disclosure are static markup. Keyboard operation of the trace is therefore drawn, not proven; only focus and hover behave.
This list is part of the deliverable, not an apology attached to one. An accessibility claim that has not been tested is a design intention, and the difference matters more here than anywhere else in the file — a future implementation agent will inherit whichever of the two it is told it is getting.

G2 · THE DIVERGENCE RULE /structure from space, structure from rules Proposed

Marketing takes its structure from space; the console takes its structure from rules. That is the whole divergence, and it is not a compromise between two design opinions — it follows from what each surface is being asked to do. A landing page for an invite-gated beta has one job, which is to make a reader believe the thing is real and then stop talking. Scarcity is the argument: five elements, long intervals, one action. Adding hairlines to that would fence off regions that have nothing to separate, and the room would start to read as a form.

A report has the opposite job. It puts ten measurements next to each other and invites the reader to compare them, which means the reader must never be able to mistake one row's number for the next row's. A hairline there is not decoration — it is the assertion that these two values are separate facts. Removing rules from that surface to make it feel more like the landing page would be trading away the only thing keeping the data honest to look at.

Hard constraint
Do not add hairlines to marketing to make it match the product. Section C carries exactly one structural rule in its entire height, above the specification strip, where the register genuinely changes. A second one has to earn itself the same way, by marking a real change of register — never by consistency argument.
Hard constraint
Do not remove structural rules from the product to make it match marketing. Every hairline in Section D separates two measurements. Deleting one does not simplify the table; it merges two facts. If the console feels crowded, remove an element — never a rule, and never the air around the type.
The test
Take any element from one surface to the other. If it needs a new colour, a new family, a new weight or a new radius to survive the trip, the two expressions have drifted apart. If it only needs different spacing and framing, they have not — that difference is the design working as intended.
The failure this rule guards against is not ugliness, it is dishonesty by borrowing: a landing page dressed as an instrument implies data it does not have, and a report dressed as a landing page implies confidence it has not earned. Each surface should look like the claim it is actually making.

G4 · HOW THIS BREAKS /the four likely failures Open
  • Hairlines migrate into marketing. The most likely failure, because it looks like tidiness. Someone lines up the specification strip in a bordered grid “to match the report”, and the closed room becomes a spreadsheet. Forbidden, explicitly, in G2.
  • Rules get deleted from the console. The second most likely, because it looks like taste. Someone strips the row hairlines for a cleaner table, and two measurements begin to read as one. Also forbidden in G2.
  • A second variant appears. A third button treatment, a second wordmark lockup, an alternative annotation voice, a display step outside the ladder. Each is small; together they are a fork. Conflict C-3 is exactly this pressure, registered before it happened.
  • A hue arrives “just for this one case”. Conflict C-2, quarantined in E5. There is no partial adoption: permit amber at Competition High and the next request is a green at Low, and the system is a traffic light on both surfaces.
Three of the four are already registered as conflicts or forbidden lists elsewhere in this file, which is the point of registering them: the ways a system loses coherence are predictable, and naming them in advance is cheaper than adjudicating them afterwards. This section resolves none of the three conflicts — C-1, C-2 and C-3 remain open Product-Lead decisions, listed in the register in Section A.
